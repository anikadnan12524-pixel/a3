Kitchen basics for everyday cooks

The essentials that make daily cooking easier: a well-stocked cupboard, simple techniques, storage times and the habits that keep food safe.

Stock up

The everyday pantry

With these on hand you can always put together a meal, even when the fridge looks empty.

  • Grains and pasta: rice, pasta, oats, couscous and noodles.
  • Tins and jars: tomatoes, beans, chickpeas, tuna, coconut milk and stock cubes.
  • Freezer: peas, spinach, berries, bread and a few portions of cooked meals.
  • Flavour: olive oil, vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, onions, lemons and dried herbs.
Skills

Six techniques worth mastering

Knife basics

Curl your fingers into a “claw” and rock the knife. Sharp knives are safer than blunt ones.

Roasting vegetables

Hot oven, a little oil, plenty of space on the tray. Crowded trays steam instead of browning.

Cooking rice

Rinse, use about 1.5 parts water to 1 of rice, cover, simmer 12 minutes and rest 10.

Seasoning

Season in stages and taste as you go. A squeeze of lemon lifts almost any dish.

Making a sauce

Soften onion and garlic, add tomatoes or stock and simmer until it coats a spoon.

Batch cooking

Cook double, cool fast, portion and label. Your future self will thank you.

Storage

How long food keeps

Cooked leftovers3–4 days2–3 months
Cooked rice1 day1 month
Raw chicken1–2 days9 months
Raw minced meat1–2 days3–4 months

General guidance based on widely used food safety advice such as the USDA’s. Always check pack dates. Frozen food stays safe for longer, but quality slowly declines.

Flavour

A starter spice shelf

  • Black pepper and sea salt for everything.
  • Ground cumin and smoked paprika for chilli, beans and roasted veg.
  • Dried oregano and thyme for sauces and tray bakes.
  • Chilli flakes for gentle heat.
  • Cinnamon for porridge, baking and warm stews.

Store spices away from the stove and replace ground spices about once a year.

Baking

Bake your own bread

A basic loaf needs only flour, water, yeast and salt. Mix 500 g strong flour, 7 g yeast, 1 tsp salt and 320 ml warm water, knead for 10 minutes, let it rise until doubled, shape, rise again and bake at 220°C (425°F) for 30–35 minutes until it sounds hollow underneath.

Homemade bread freezes beautifully: slice it first so you can toast straight from frozen.
