      A good day of eating

      A simple daily rhythm

      There’s no single right way to eat, but regular, varied meals make cooking and shopping much easier. Here’s a relaxed pattern many households find works.

      Morning

      Breakfast

      Something with fibre and protein: oats, eggs on toast or yogurt with fruit.

      Midday

      Lunch

      Leftovers, a big salad with beans or a filled wrap. Cook once, eat twice.

      Evening

      Dinner

      Half the plate vegetables, plus protein and a whole grain or potato.

      In between

      Snacks

      Fruit, nuts, cheese and crackers or vegetable sticks with hummus.

      Shopping

      Shop once, cook all week

      • Check before you list. Look in the fridge, freezer and cupboards first.
      • Group your list by aisle so you’re in and out quickly.
      • Buy ingredients that cross over, like peppers for a stir-fry and a pasta sauce.
      • Keep a stocked cupboard of tins, grains, spices and frozen veg for backup meals.
      • Shop after eating, never hungry, to avoid impulse buys.
      Food safety

      Four everyday habits

      • Clean: wash hands, boards and surfaces before and after handling raw food.
      • Separate: use different boards for raw meat and ready-to-eat food.
      • Cook: chicken to 74°C (165°F), minced meat to 71°C (160°F).
      • Chill: keep the fridge at 4°C (40°F) or below and refrigerate leftovers within two hours.

      Questions

      Everyday cooking FAQ

      How do I start meal planning?

      Start small. Plan four or five dinners for the coming week, check what you already have, and write one shopping list. Leave a night or two free for leftovers or eating out. Once that feels easy, add lunches.

      How much food should I cook per person?

      A useful guide for an adult dinner is about 100–150 g of cooked protein, 75 g of dry pasta or rice, and at least two big handfuls of vegetables. Appetites vary, so adjust for your household.

      How long do leftovers last in the fridge?

      Most cooked leftovers are best eaten within three to four days. Cool them quickly, refrigerate within two hours and reheat until steaming hot all the way through.

      Can I freeze cooked meals?

      Yes. Soups, stews, sauces, cooked grains and many bakes freeze well for two to three months. Freeze in portions, label with the date and defrost in the fridge overnight.

      What’s the easiest way to save time on weeknights?

      Batch-cook basics such as rice, a tray of roasted vegetables and a big pot of sauce at the weekend, then mix and match them into quick meals during the week.
